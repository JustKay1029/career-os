## D. Relationship Pipeline

### 1. College peers and seniors
- **Where:** IILM CSE WhatsApp/Discord groups, your own batch, LinkedIn "People you may know" filtered to IILM
- **How to choose:** Seniors who've done internships, hackathons, or open-source work — check their LinkedIn activity first
- **First message:**
  > "Hi [Name], I'm Kavya, 2nd-year CSE at IILM. Saw you [did X internship/posted about Y] — I'm working toward something similar in AI/ML. Would love to know how you approached it, if you have a few minutes sometime."
- **Follow-up (1 week later if no reply):**
  > "Hi [Name], following up in case this got buried — no worries if you're busy!"
- **Value-first:** Share a resource, offer to review their project README, or invite them to your GitHub repo as a second pair of eyes
- **Referral timing:** Only after 2–3 genuine exchanges, and only if they've seen your actual work

### 2. IILM Gurgaon alumni
- **Where:** LinkedIn Alumni tool (Company page → search "IILM University" or "IILM Gurugram" → click "Alumni")
- **Filters:** Filter by "Where they work" (target companies) and "What they studied" (CSE)
- **How to choose:** Alumni 2–5 years out, in AI/ML/SWE roles — closer to your stage, more likely to respond
- **First message:**
  > "Hi [Name], I'm a CSE student at IILM Gurugram. I noticed you graduated from here and are now at [Company] — I'm exploring AI/ML and would really value hearing how your path went, especially anything you'd do differently as a student."
- **Follow-up:** Same as above, sent after 7–10 days
- **Value-first:** Ask a specific, well-researched question (not "any tips?") — shows you did homework
- **Referral timing:** Only after they've responded warmly to at least one real exchange, and only when you're a genuine fit for an open role

### 3. Open-source maintainers/contributors
- **Where:** GitHub repos tagged `good-first-issue`, GSSoC/Hacktoberfest-participating repos, Twitter/X OSS threads
- **How to choose:** Repos with active maintainers (recent commits/replies), clear contribution guidelines
- **First interaction (NOT a DM — a public issue comment):**
  > "Hi, I'd like to work on this if it's still open. I was thinking of approaching it by [brief plan]. Does that align with what you had in mind, or is there context I'm missing?"
- **After a merged PR (follow-up):**
  > "Thanks for the review and the merge! Learned a lot from this — is there a good next issue for someone at my level, or an area you'd recommend I look into next?"
- **Turning it into a relationship:** Keep contributing to the *same* 1–2 repos rather than spreading thin — repeated, recognizable presence builds real rapport with maintainers

### 4. Engineers in Delhi NCR (AI/ML, Python, backend, OSS)
- **Where:** LinkedIn search, Delhi NCR tech meetup groups (see Section G)
- **How to choose:** People who post technical content (not just job updates) — signals they'll actually engage
- **First message:**
  > "Hi [Name], I came across your post on [specific topic] — [one genuine reaction/question]. I'm a CS student building in the same space and would love to learn from people working on this in NCR."
- **Referral timing:** Not in scope until you have a real project or contribution to point to and they've engaged with your work

### 5. Recruiters/founders
- **Only engage when:** You have a live application, a referral-ready portfolio, or they're hiring for something you're a genuine fit for
- **Template (Section I covers this in full)**

## E. LinkedIn Search Workflow (Boolean strings)

1. **IILM Gurgaon alumni in software/AI roles:**
   - Go to IILM's LinkedIn company page → "Alumni" tab → filter by "What they studied": Computer Science
   - Search bar string: `"IILM" AND ("software engineer" OR "machine learning" OR "AI engineer")`

2. **Alumni at target companies:**
   - On the target company's LinkedIn page → People tab → search: `"IILM University"` in the "Schools" filter

3. **Engineers in AI/ML, Python, backend, OSS in Delhi NCR:**
   - LinkedIn search: `("machine learning engineer" OR "backend developer" OR "python developer") AND ("Gurugram" OR "Delhi NCR" OR "Noida")`
   - Add filter: 2nd-degree connections first (higher response rate)

4. **Students/early-career preparing for GSoC:**
   - Search: `"GSoC" AND ("student" OR "contributor")` — then filter location to India
   - Also check LinkedIn/Twitter hashtags: #GSoC2027, #OpenSource, #GSSoC

## F. Open-Source Networking Workflow (repeatable, weekly)

1. **Find repos:** GitHub search `label:"good first issue" language:Python stars:>500 pushed:>2026-07-01` — active, legitimate, not abandoned
2. **Read before claiming:** Read the last 10 comments on the issue — has someone already claimed it? Is the maintainer responsive?
3. **Comment before coding** (template in Section D3)
4. **Ask focused questions**, not "how do I start":
   > "I noticed [specific function] handles X — should the fix live there, or is there a reason it's separated from Y?"
5. **After merge:** Send the follow-up template (Section D3), and star/watch the repo to stay visible in its activity
6. **Non-annoying relationship building:** Comment on the maintainer's *other* repos or posts occasionally — genuine interest, not constant pinging

## G. Event Workflow (Gurugram/Delhi NCR)

- **Platforms to monitor:** Meetup.com, Luma (lu.ma), GDG Delhi/Gurugram, Devfolio (hackathons), Insider.in (tech track), college/company tech meetup pages
- **10-min weekly search routine (do this in your Saturday/Sunday block):** Search "AI ML meetup Gurugram," "Python meetup Delhi NCR," "GDG Gurugram," "open source meetup Delhi" on Meetup + Luma
- **Worth attending if:** Free/low-cost, technical (not purely networking-mixer), speakers/attendees list shows people in your target companies/roles
- **Pre-event plan:** Pick 2–3 people from the attendee list (if visible) or speaker lineup; prepare 1 specific question for each based on their work
- **Conversation plan:** Lead with a genuine question about their talk/work, not your own pitch; only share your background if asked
- **24-hour follow-up:**
  > "Hi [Name], great meeting you at [event] — really liked what you said about [specific point]. Would love to stay in touch on LinkedIn."
- **Contact tracker template:** Name | Where met | What we discussed | Follow-up sent (Y/N) | Next step
- **Goal:** 2–3 real conversations per event, not a stack of business cards

## I. Referral Readiness

**Checklist — all must be true before asking:**
- [ ] You've had at least 2–3 genuine exchanges with this person
- [ ] They've seen actual proof of your work (project, PR, post)
- [ ] You're not just "interested" in the company — you have a specific, current opening in mind

**Outreach template (internship/job-specific):**
> "Hi [Name], I saw [Company] has an opening for [role] — I've been working on [specific relevant project/contribution], and it looks like a strong fit for what I'm building toward. I know referral requests can be a lot to ask, so no pressure at all, but if you felt comfortable, I'd really appreciate a referral. Happy to send my resume either way, and totally understand if it's not the right fit."

**Easy-decline framing (already built into the above):** "no pressure at all... totally understand if it's not the right fit."

**Thank-you/update template:**
> "Thank you again for [referring me / the conversation] — wanted to give you a quick update: [outcome]. Really appreciate you taking the time, regardless of how it goes."

**Relationship maintenance rule:** Whether you're selected or not, send the thank-you within 48 hours, and check in genuinely (not just when you need something) every 1–2 months — share something you built, not just updates on your job search.
